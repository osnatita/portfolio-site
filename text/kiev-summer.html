Kiev, Summer

קייב, קיץ

Osnat Ita Skoblinski · Eretz Haemori, 19 September 2013

Break — that is the default setting of every clerk, shopkeeper, salesgirl, information officer, waitress, cigarette seller. Not a trace of a smile at the corner of their mouths, only a twitch of the lips and an expression of disgust. There are no customers here and no gentlemen, only railway stations, monstrous and predatory, grey and drunk. And inside, islands of grace: a grand hall of wood and glass, royal toilets from the days of a baron and a baroness, at their door a turnstile arm that turns at the command of sullen coins. And inside — a stinking hole in a decorated ceramic floor.

Faster, please, scolds the human train behind me. From the bus window I witness the remains of a post-Soviet volcanic eruption, the lava dried and set into the shape of enormous monuments, ridges of buildings, an architectural masterpiece of perfect ugliness, cathedrals dreary and full of majesty. The woman beside me, the outline of her lips drawn in a coarse pink, her hair straw, her finger digging in her nose, her belongings bursting out of printed plastic bags and falling into a river whose bridges throw up concrete spires. A door opens, and at the end of it stairs going down through the belly of the earth, straight into its arsehole, and at the centre of that an eye looking back at you. Kiev, you are a stomach ulcer, you are mountains of churches, you are gold ornament, you are trays of borscht and men in rings, you are a bridge that refuses to collapse although the banks have already dried. You are gardens and rivers, you are heaps of rubbish, you wish me long life and shove me straight into the airport.

Translator's note

קייב, את כיב קיבה — Kiev, at kiv keiva. The name of the city and the Hebrew for a stomach ulcer are almost the same sound. The English keeps the image and loses the rhyme.
