Two Poems

שני שירים

Osnat Ita Skoblinski · Montreal, 2015

Beyond the railway tracks, between the treetops stretches the enormous grey building of Home Depot inside it, between the aisles, toilets and potted plants rest quietly I wanted to stretch out on the tracks, to rest among the reddish shards of a car's lights to get inside the dented lid of a jar that had taken me captive

A tiredness I had never known came over me, came down on me as an order to cease, or to be I would have deposited myself in the bank of energy, released between waterfalls harnessed at the force of concrete at the Canadian power station, I would have run like a current into television and computer screens in houses with their windows shut forgetting the war of attrition that wraps itself through layers of necessity and coffee gone cold

Upstairs the music stopped and I knew the painter was still standing there as he had stood all day facing the big wall, at the same angle he had stood at that morning

You never came to visit me, and the seed of strangeness scattered a dread only immigrants know.

At night the furniture began to multiply in one violent stroke. The chairs covered themselves over and grew legs like stars. The drawers migrated documents and bills out of themselves and filled themselves again and again. The sofas crowded in beneath themselves, into the floor tiles. The beds collapsed upwards into tower blocks, their mattresses pressed to the ceiling. He woke on the third mattress between the new heaven and the new earth and looked at all of it as though he had expected it to be there. An enormous relief washed through him. For his thoughts, which kept scattering out into the space, there was no room; they were pressed up against him, torn loose, desperate, unanchored. He lay down with a faint sigh and stared at the underside of the bed above him. Parts of wood and metal found their new place and rested, still, exactly as they had rested before, exactly as he was resting now.

Translator's notes

The first poem turns three times on one root: פקדה אותי, פקדה עליי, מפקידה: a tiredness that visits, commands, and is deposited like money. English can hold the three senses only by spelling them out.

Its last line runs וזרע הזרות זרה אימה, seed, strangeness, scattered, three words off the same sound. Only the sense survives.

In the second poem the drawers הגירו their contents: in Hebrew a drawer, מגירה, and emigration, הגירה, are the same letters. Given the first poem, that is not an accident.
