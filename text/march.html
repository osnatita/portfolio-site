March

צעדה

Osnat Ita Skoblinski · Zuta, issue 149 · Haokets, 13 November 2010

The backsides of the women in power pull tight in their uniforms their batons gleaming their smile nervous their legs all jitter They do not understand why we are demonstrating, or how the two who are fighting carry the same flag, or why the humour goes no further than the embarrassed smile of the ones repeating after the young man screaming from a sheet of paper into an enormous megaphone, or why the police have a megaphone so small and so useless, or why the men whose beards grow on their shirts wrap the whole thing in a screen of smoke.

Behind them they are selling literature by the kilo and sad covers wrap around labouring words

The screaming young man, his fingers trembling I imagine his blaze on my body

Translator's note

The Hebrew title, צעדה, is the noun, a march or a procession, not the month and not the verb.

The closing word, להטו, is his heat, his flame, his blaze; the same root gives the flaming sword that guards Eden. Two versions of the poem exist, in Zuta and in Haokets, differing by a few words. This follows the Zuta text.
