Reporting, argument, essays and poems written between 2009 and 2022, first from Tel Aviv, then from Montreal, in Hebrew and in English.

Pieces written in Hebrew are marked, and each has a full English translation hosted here, with a link to the Hebrew source at the top of the page. Articles · seven

Reported journalism.

His Flock in the Holy Land

Haaretz·2009·with Yuval Ben-Ami

Israel's Catholic congregation numbered a few thousand until migrant workers swelled it by more than twenty thousand. The piece opens at Saint Anthony's in Jaffa, in the hour when the Arabic mass hands the pews over to the English one and the Filipina caregivers slip into the emptied seats.

Poles Apart

Haaretz·2009·with Yuval Ben-Ami

The Israeli guides who walk teenagers through the death camps, on a trip of their own, this time to meet the country that the usual itinerary reduces to a backdrop.

JetLAG of Cultures

Shtetl Montreal·2015

A Russian-initiated music festival in the Catskills, unexpectedly full of klezmer. Psoy Korolenko and the ethnographer Michael Alpert on why Yiddish culture survived Soviet Russia partly by being ignored, and on what the New York-Moscow-Tel Aviv triangle sounds like.

On the Road, Again ממשיכה את דרכו

Ynet·2009·Translated from Hebrew

Chapter one of a seven-part series retracing Kerouac's route across America fifty years late, with very little money and a copy of the book. First stop, San Francisco, where the Beats are not to be found and the search is itself the journey.

Read the translation/המקור בעברית

Horror Films and an Addiction to Gasoline: Indigenous Life in Canada's North סרטי אימה והתמכרות לדלק: החיים של הילידים בצפון קנדה

Haaretz·2018·Translated from Hebrew

A week in Yellowknife, at latitude 62: a horror-film festival born of boredom, a museum’s count of the languages Canada has left, teenagers drinking by a frozen lake at noon, and an anthropologist the school system had written off explaining how he got out. It ends on the northern lights and a DNA test.

Read the translation/המקור בעברית

A Hole in the Bagel חור בבייגל

2016·Hebrew manuscript

A food historian’s walking tour of Jewish Montreal: St-Viateur against Fairmount, a steakhouse won in a poker game in 1938, Mrs Whyte handing out pickles instead of sweets on Halloween, and a back alley with nothing left in it at all, which turns out to be the most moving stop on the route.

Read the translation

Blame Canada תאשימו את קנדה

2016·Hebrew manuscript

Written six months into Justin Trudeau’s first term, while Americans were googling how to emigrate. An adviser on the left on the selfie and who actually pays for it, a reckoning with Canada’s little-sister complex, and a long look at Vancouver, Toronto, and the city that went broke and became a music scene.

Op-eds · four

Arguments, made in public.

My Jewish Family Is Being Bombed in Ukraine

The Forward·2022

“Sometimes it feels like history is doing its best to prove to my family that there's no safe place on earth.” Written in the first week of the invasion, with an aunt and two cousins sitting in the dark in Kharkiv so as not to be targeted. One of them went outside, at the risk of his life, to buy bread.

There Are No ‘Two Sides’ to the Israeli-Palestinian Conflict

FairPlanet·2021

Written from the position of having friends among both the occupier and the occupied, and refusing the symmetry the phrase implies: the two are not equal in power, in agency, in military force, or in the count of the dead.

Archived copy/Author page

I Was Taught to Hate Palestinians — Until I Met One

+972 Magazine·2017

“I grew up believing the Arabs wanted to throw us into the sea.” On meeting one young man from Gaza in her twenties, and on the slow, unglamorous work of taking apart everything that came before it.

Zero Decency: Nissan Shor Doesn't Really Regret What He Wrote About Me אפס בהגינות: ניסן שור לא באמת מתחרט על מה שכתב עליי

Haaretz·2017·Translated from Hebrew

Nine years after going public against the man who assaulted her, and after a columnist called her vulgar and said she should be sued, an answer to his eventual apology, which turns out to be a column about clearing his own name. It ends with practical advice for women about to complain, and for anyone who wants to stand behind them.

Essays · six

First person, at length.

Neither Russian nor Israeli: Lessons from a Journey to the Homeland

+972 Magazine·2014

Four phases of an identity: refusing the Russian one as a child, adopting it as a teenager out of spite, reading her way back into the language, and then going to Kharkiv with her parents and being told she would only ever count as Jewish. An argument for societies that can hold a mishmash without ranking it.

How the Man Who Sexually Harassed Me Ended Up With a Black Eye

BUST Magazine

The incident that Israeli media turned into a sensation, told by the person it happened to, and what followed when she declined to appear on the talk shows that wanted to debate whether she had responded correctly.

Read at BUST/Archived copy

I Hate Montreal — the Best City in the World אני שונאת את מונטריאול - העיר הכי טובה בעולם

Haaretz·2018·Translated from Hebrew

A refusal to write the piece, and then the piece: an argument by anaphora against the best-kept secret in North America. Nobody left to take your anger out on, no excuse left for not fixing your own bike, no excuse left for not making the work. “You come here because you can breathe, and it is terribly boring, breathing all the time.”

Haaretz·2018·Translated from Hebrew

Saying the wrong thing out loud at a Neil Young concert in Canada, and then being converted. Jane Birkin at seventy-one, leaning on a piano in Converse. And a fifteen-minute walk from one hill to another that lands her in a different world entirely.

Nine numbered fragments from Pop Montreal: music venues as the houses of faith we have left, FOMO as a spiritual problem, a congregation surrendering for two hours to Godspeed You! Black Emperor in a converted church, and two panels on what it still costs to be a woman at the decks.

Poetry · six

Hebrew, 2009–2015.

The City Changed

Hotem·Women issue·written in English

“The city changed. The mountains became sea, the cold whisper of war turned into the stale warm weather of sex and dog feces […] I didn't want to write poetry, I still refuse, but how do you translate fear?”

Kiev, Summer קייב, קיץ

Eretz Haemori·2013·Translated from Hebrew

A prose poem written on the 2013 trip to Ukraine. Railway stations “monstrous and predatory, grey and drunk,” royal toilets with a stinking hole in the decorated floor, and a city addressed directly, in the second person, until it shoves her into the airport.

March צעדה

Zuta, issue 149·Haokets, 2010·Translated from Hebrew

A demonstration, seen from inside it: policewomen who do not understand why anyone is shouting, a megaphone too small to be useful, literature sold by the kilo behind the line, and a young man whose fingers shake as he screams from a sheet of paper.

Seven poems written inside Twitter's 140 characters, and published as poems: airports with no departing flights, broken bridges that lead from a river back to the same river, an opera projected onto solar panels.

Home Depot seen from the railway tracks, and a night when the furniture starts to multiply. Two poems in which the Hebrew keeps folding drawers into emigration, and tiredness into a sum of money deposited at a bank.
