Cooking Kubbeh Khamusteh

Ita Skoblinski · from Montreal, Under Construction

In the beginning there wasn't light.

The dancing bush left wooden veins on the building's top corner. I could no longer imagine the soft movement of its leaves rattling and caressing it with the summer's breeze.

The skies were replaced by one immense cloud which became our universe. Reflecting its whiteness in the fresh snow.

The windows stood helpless, not admitting any lights or shapes. Useless as your lack of admittance, as your overwhelmed indifference.

I cook, as the outcome of the process seems comforting. As the dough submits to my touch, to my whims. My fingers demonstrate a confidence I never held. If it burns or breaks or sucks, I am the only one to blame.

The kitchen is dark, and the turmeric soup shines like the sun. I stir it as my will stands still.

I become a dumpling.

About the project

Montreal, Under Construction sets out what it is doing in its own opening: relocating to a city is not the same as immigrating to a country. One is bureaucratic, the other is the street your house is on. The early texts look outward at the setting, and the later ones turn inward, until the city has become a form of identity, "as fluid as a dress you can take off and as solid as the bricks that make up your four walls."

The poem directly after this one in the manuscript, Examination, is the immigration medical: a doctor tapping her knee on behalf of a state deciding whether she may call the city home.
